Date: arXiv v1, 6 Nov 2026. Code: github.com/wwillchen/ebr. Project: energy-based-reasoners.github.io

## 1. What the method is

**Setting.** Algorithmic reasoning (x, y) on small fixed-size boards/graphs (Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, N-Queens, Graph Coloring). A looped reasoner refines one latent z_t, z_{t+1} = f_θ(z_t, x).

**Energy network (Eq. 7, App. A.1).**
- Backbone: the TRM/EqR recurrent block, 2 Transformer blocks, 4-head self-attention (non-causal, over all puzzle tokens), SwiGLU MLP, RMSNorm, 2D RoPE. Input h = f_θ(z + emb(x)), tokens scaled by sqrt(d).
- Energy head: a shared per-token linear head (W, b, w) reduces each token's output to a scalar. E_θ(z; x) = Σ_i ϕ(head(h_i)), with ϕ = identity except softplus on Maze-Hard. The head adds d²+2d parameters (66k at d=256, 263k at d=512).
- Readout: a linear layer decodes the prediction from each token's latent.

**Update rule (Alg. 2).** Projected noisy gradient descent on the energy:
- z̃ = RMS(z) + σ·ξ, ξ ~ N(0, I)
- z ← Π_M( z̃ − α ∇_M E_θ(z̃; x) )
- σ = 0.4 → 0.05 (Sudoku), 0.3 (Queens, Coloring), 0.4 (Maze). Projection Π_M onto a sphere.
- The step size α and step count n are sampled per training segment (randomized descent). Evaluation uses fixed n and α.

**Training (Alg. 1, Eq. 3–5).**
- Segment-wise deep supervision: S segments of n steps. Gradients flow only through the last H steps (truncated BPTT). Requires second-order derivatives through the energy gradient.
- Loss: L = Σ_{t=n−H+1..n} w_t · stablemax-CE(z_t, y) + λ·L_conv.
- Convergence residual r_n = mean over last H steps of ||z_t − z_{t−1}|| / ||z_{t−1}||.
- L_conv = BCE( σ((δ − r_n)/κ), 1[correct] ), κ = 0.05. Teaches: converged states should be correct. Halting when r_n < δ (δ = 0.25 Sudoku/Queens/Coloring, 0.75 Maze).
- Stop training segments when converged.

**Exploration: SXM (state-based Explorative Modeling, Sec. 3.2).**
- Roll out K candidate trajectories per training row (K=2 on Sudoku and Queens/Coloring, 1 on Maze).
- Forward SXM: keep the lowest-loss trajectory. Reverse SXM: pair each trajectory with its closest valid solution (needs the solution set; used on Queens and Coloring).
- Trajectories are supervised at their intermediate states.

**Optimization (App. A.2).** Muon for hidden matrices (momentum 0.95, 5 Newton–Schulz steps), AdamW (β=0.9, 0.95) for embeddings, readout, and vectors. Base lr 1e-4, warmup 100–1000 steps, wd 0.1, batch 768 (64 for Maze), EMA 0.999. 30k–70k steps.

**Trained vs training-free.** Everything is trained from scratch on the task data. No pretrained LM, no teacher, no distillation, no training-free component.

**Compute.** One H200 per seed: 3 h (Sudoku), 3 h (Maze), 4–8 h (Queens), 5–8 h (Coloring). Second-order gradients needed custom GPU kernels (shipped in their repo). Inference per puzzle: 0.0129 TFLOPs per iteration (one segment of 21 descent steps, each a forward + backward). Early stopping at ||∇E|| < 0.2 gives 0.060 TFLOPs/puzzle at 16 iterations.

## 2. Reported results

Sudoku-Extreme (exact accuracy, 422,786 test puzzles, 1.8M params):
- EBR N=1: 99.4% (single-sample table). Ablation run: 98.96%.
- EBR N=8: 99.9%. Compared with TRM 92.1% (320 iter, N=1) and GRAM 97.4% at N=50.
- Inference FLOPs: 345× fewer than the next-best model at comparable accuracy (Fig. 5b). Training FLOPs 2.6× fewer.

Maze-Hard (1.8M params): 87.4% single sample, 88.3% multi-sample.

N-Queens and Graph Coloring (7.1M params; GRAM datasets):
- 8×8 Queens N=1: 99.51%-100% accuracy vs GRAM 99.5%.
- 10×10 Queens coverage Cov@20: 92.0% vs GRAM 57.5%. Note: their coverage metric differs from GRAM's; the report says GRAM's definition gives 89.3%.
- Graph Coloring: conflicts 0 (EBR) vs 2.7–3.3 (GRAM). Coverage numbers are metric-dependent.

Ablations (Sudoku N=1): remove energy head → 60.05% (from 98.96%); fixed α → 97.59%; remove convergence loss → 45.32%; replace L_conv with a q-head → 92.42%; SXM K=2 → 99.36%.

Baselines: Looped Transformer, HRM (27M), TRM (7M), GRAM (10M), EqR (5–7M). All are small algorithmic reasoners.

## 3. Tested on looped, hybrid, or GQA/MLA models?

- **Looped:** Yes, but only tiny looped recurrent reasoners (1.8M–7.1M params, hidden 256/512) on algorithmic tasks. Not a pretrained looped LM.
- **Hybrid linear attention (Gated DeltaNet):** No.
- **GQA / MLA / any KV-cache architecture:** No. The backbone is full bidirectional self-attention over one puzzle, with no KV cache, no autoregressive decoding, and no cross-loop cache.
- **Language modeling:** No. Section 5 says results are "currently limited to structured algorithmic reasoning tasks" and that scaling to language is open.

## 4. Fit for SenseNovaKV

Goal by goal:

1. **KV size (DeepSeek-class <1 KB/token, FP4, sharing):** No contribution. The paper has no KV cache, no latent, no quantization. Estimated gain: 0 bytes/token.
2. **Quality preservation in distillation:** No contribution. The paper trains from scratch on task labels; it has no teacher, no layer-local matching, and no knowledge-retention evaluation. Its convergence loss is a fixed-point correctness signal, not a distillation loss. Estimated gain: none.
3. **Kernels / speed on T4 with 8 GB:** Negative. Energy descent needs a forward and backward pass through the backbone per descent step, plus second-order gradients. Applying this to the 9B model would multiply per-token compute by the number of descent steps (their segment uses 21 descent steps). That conflicts directly with the decode-speed goal. Estimate: ≥10× per-visit compute, unless only applied in a tiny head (not tested).
4. **Looped efficiency:** Partial idea only. The useful piece is the convergence criterion r_n = relative latent change, used as an adaptive-exit signal, and the claim that convergence should be supervised with correctness. Related work already tests loop exits on real LMs (Ouro, Ragged KV 2610.09013, RIPE/"Recency is the wrong axis" 2610.34436, LoopKV 2611.04137), and those are more directly relevant. Estimated gain on our model: unknown; any gain would be small compared with what our KV path needs.

**Conflicts:**
- Energy-based recurrence needs the whole block re-trained with a new head and second-order gradients. This conflicts with our plan to freeze FFN and DeltaNet weights and train only the attention-side latent path.
- Non-causal, puzzle-level attention does not map to causal LM decoding. A per-token latent that is refined by descent before the next token is generated is not defined in our autoregressive setting.
- Each energy-descent step adds a backward pass, which is incompatible with the 2×T4 and 8 GB budget.

## 5. Implementation plan

**Recommended: none for the engine or distillation.** The paper does not change the KV design, the kernels, or the distillation objective. Do not add EBR to kaggle/distill/distill.py, engine/kernels/*, or engine/runtime/snkv_engine.py.

If the convergence signal is worth a diagnostic (low priority), the cheapest check is training-free and uses existing infrastructure:
- File: kaggle/kv_analysis/kv_analysis.py. Add a forward hook on each full-attention layer's input to record, per token, the relative change between visit 0 and visit 1 of the same physical layer, r = ||h_visit1 − h_visit0|| / ||h_visit0||.
- Measure on Preview weights, 64 WikiText-2 and 64 fineweb-edu sequences × 2,048 tokens (the existing kv_analysis setup). Report: Spearman correlation of r with per-token NLL change when visit 1 is skipped (replace it with visit 0's output), and the fraction of tokens with r below τ = 0.05, 0.1, 0.25.
- Decision rule: if skipping visit 1 costs < 1% PPL on tokens with r < τ covering > 40% of tokens, an adaptive-exit experiment is worth doing. Otherwise drop it. Expected compute: one kv_analysis run, a few GPU-hours on one T4.

## 6. Verdict

**SKIP.** The paper studies energy-based recurrent reasoners trained from scratch on 1.8M–7.1M-parameter algorithmic puzzles. It has no KV cache, no language modeling, no distillation, and no GQA, MLA, or hybrid model. Its only transferable piece, the relative-change convergence signal, is already covered for real LMs by Ouro, Ragged KV, and RIPE, and is cheaper to test with the training-free kv_analysis diagnostic above.
